Why AI visibility matters

AI Visibility

AI is becoming part of trip planning before a traveler ever reaches a booking site. When people ask where to stay in a city, near an attraction or along a highway, the hotels that surface in the answer are the ones that get considered, and often the ones that get booked.

Technical quick audit

The site is open to AI crawlers. The opportunity is in what its pages say about location.

We spot-checked robots.txt, a hotel page, three city pages and three landmark pages, comparing the raw HTML a crawler receives with the page a visitor sees, plus part of a site crawl. [clientsite.com] is a very large site, so this is a starting read: it needs a full technical audit of its own, which is part of the full GEO audit.

Crawler access

PASS
robots.txt explicitly allows OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User and PerplexityBot, and does not block GPTBot, ClaudeBot or Google-Extended. The engine differences in section 04 are not an access problem.

Content citability

FAIL
AI cites [clientsite.com] hotel pages, not the city and landmark pages built to answer “where should I stay” questions. Of the 25 [clientsite.com] pages AI cited most, 16 are individual hotel pages and none is a city or landmark page. Those pages are hard to quote:
  • Landmark pages don’t name the landmark. The [Landmark] page’s title and H1 say “290 [Street]” and never “[Landmark].”
  • Lists aren’t local. After the first hotel, the [Landmark] list jumps to hotels 16 to 26 km away; the [City 5] city page mixes in [Nearby city], [Nearby city], [Nearby city] and [Nearby city].
  • Duplicate pages compete. The Air [Country] Centre and [Arena] pages are identical and both point to themselves as the main version.
  • The answers travelers ask for are missing. City and hotel pages have no FAQ, and only one nearby attraction on the [Client brand A] [Landmark area] page has a walking time.

Rendering

PARTIAL
Hotel descriptions and amenities are in the raw HTML. City page headings and the distances on landmark pages only appear after JavaScript runs, so crawlers that read raw HTML miss them. The [Client brand A] [Landmark area] page is 3.6 MB of HTML for about 4,300 characters of visible text.

Structured data

PARTIAL
Hotel pages have strong Hotel markup (address, location and amenities such as EV charging, free hot breakfast and pools), but no ratings, price range or check-in time. City and landmark pages mark up each hotel by name only, with no address, so names like “[Client brand B] & Suites” are ambiguous.

Internal links

PARTIAL
Sitemaps are declared and hotel pages point to clean main versions. A partial crawl surfaced malformed internal links (doubled slashes and external addresses appended to site paths), links that redirect to insecure http:// addresses, and broken links, including /faq.
Status reflects a limited sample; the full technical audit validates each finding sitewide.
Quick wins

Three opportunities we can see before the full audit.

Each one comes straight from the findings above. The full audit validates and prioritizes them.

Quick win 01 · Owned content

Put real content on the pages AI already cites.

Of the 25 [clientsite.com] pages AI cited most, 16 are individual hotel pages and the rest are the home, loyalty, deals and business-travel pages. None is a city or landmark page. Add what travelers ask about to the hotel pages AI reads (nearby landmarks with distances, pet policy, parking, breakfast, [League] game-day details), and give city and landmark pages content worth citing: name the landmark, put distances in the raw HTML, lead with nearby hotels, and add a short “best for” note and FAQ (see section 08). The full audit maps every page AI cites so new content goes where it expands what AI understands about [Client].

Quick win 02 · Earned authority

Make the listings and reviews Gemini and Claude trust tell the full [Client] story.

Gemini’s top sources are Expedia, Kayak, Hotels.com and Booking.com; Claude’s are Facebook, Tripadvisor, Expedia and Booking.com. For the 13 sampled hotels, complete amenity details (pet-friendly, EV charging, pools, free breakfast), photos and neighborhood descriptions on those sites, and respond to reviews consistently, starting with [Client brand C] Downtown [City 4] and the [Client brand C] and [Client brand B] brands. Both engines also cite BringFido for pet travel.

Quick win 03 · Stories [Client] already owns

Close the pet-friendly and [League] gaps in [City 4], [City 13] and [City 11].

AI named no [Client] hotel for pet-friendly stays in [City 4] or [City 13], though [Client brand A] [City 4] East and [Client brand A] Suites Downtown [City 13] are on [Client]’s own pet-friendly pages, and none near [Stadium] or [Stadium] ([City 11]). State the pet policy and the [League] partnership in plain text on those hotels’ pages and listings, and point game-day content at the hotels nearest each stadium.

Report methodology

This directional AI Snapshot uses 235 curated prompts (173 non-branded, 62 branded) run from [Country] in English across Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, Gemini, ChatGPT and Claude, using answers from November 12 to 13, 2026. Prompts are organized in layers: national (43), regional (7), city (58, including [League] game and pet-friendly questions), hotel area and named hotel (115), and brand comparisons (12), covering 13 markets with one sampled [Client] hotel each. Non-branded prompts never mention [Client], any [Client] brand or any [Client] hotel; any prompt that does is treated as branded. Neighborhood, landmark, attraction, [League], pet-friendly and brand reputation prompts were added on November 13 and reflect one run, so treat individual results for those questions as directional. The markets and hotel-area questions were chosen where [Client] operates, which favors [Client]; the full audit compares every market on equal terms.
